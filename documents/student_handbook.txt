1. UNIVERSITY OVERVIEW

Pinnacle State University (PSU) was founded in 1962 and is located in Cedar Valley, Colorado. PSU is a public research university with an enrollment of approximately 18,500 students across 6 colleges and 72 academic programs. The university is accredited by the Higher Learning Commission (HLC) and holds specialized accreditation in Engineering, Nursing, Business, and Education.

Our Mission:
To advance knowledge, inspire critical thinking, and prepare students to become ethical leaders and engaged citizens in a diverse, global society.

Our Core Values:
- Academic Excellence: We pursue the highest standards of teaching, research, and scholarship
- Integrity: We uphold honesty, transparency, and ethical behavior in all endeavors
- Inclusivity: We celebrate diversity and foster a welcoming environment for all
- Innovation: We encourage creative problem-solving and forward-thinking approaches
- Community: We strengthen bonds between students, faculty, staff, and our surrounding region

Colleges and Schools:
- College of Arts & Sciences
- College of Engineering & Technology
- College of Business Administration
- College of Health Sciences & Nursing
- College of Education
- Graduate School

2. ACADEMIC POLICIES

2.1 Enrollment and Registration
Students register for courses through the MyPSU student portal at https://my.pinnaclestate.edu. Registration opens according to the following priority schedule:
- Graduate students and seniors (90+ credits): Week 1
- Juniors (60–89 credits): Week 2
- Sophomores (30–59 credits): Week 3
- Freshmen (0–29 credits): Week 4

The add/drop period extends through the first 10 business days of each semester. After this period, course withdrawals are subject to the "W" grade policy.

2.2 Grading Scale
Pinnacle State University uses the following grading scale:
- A  (4.0): Exceptional mastery of course material
- A- (3.7): Excellent performance
- B+ (3.3): Very good performance
- B  (3.0): Good performance
- B- (2.7): Above average performance
- C+ (2.3): Satisfactory performance
- C  (2.0): Adequate performance
- C- (1.7): Below average performance
- D+ (1.3): Poor performance
- D  (1.0): Minimal passing performance
- F  (0.0): Failure to meet course requirements

Incomplete (I) grades may be assigned when a student is unable to complete coursework due to documented circumstances beyond their control. An Incomplete must be resolved within 60 calendar days of the end of the semester, or it converts to an F.

2.3 GPA Requirements
- Good Academic Standing: Cumulative GPA of 2.0 or above
- Dean's List: Semester GPA of 3.5 or above with at least 12 credit hours
- Academic Probation: Cumulative GPA below 2.0 for one semester
- Academic Suspension: Cumulative GPA below 2.0 for two consecutive semesters
- President's Honor Roll: Semester GPA of 3.9 or above with at least 15 credit hours

2.4 Academic Integrity
Pinnacle State University takes academic integrity extremely seriously. Violations include:
- Plagiarism: Submitting another's work as your own without proper attribution
- Cheating: Using unauthorized materials, information, or devices during exams
- Fabrication: Inventing or falsifying data, citations, or information
- Facilitation: Helping another student commit an academic integrity violation
- Multiple Submission: Submitting the same work for credit in multiple courses without permission

First Offense: Zero on the assignment and mandatory Academic Integrity Workshop
Second Offense: Failure (F) in the course and notation on academic record
Third Offense: Suspension or expulsion from the university

All violations are reported to the Office of Academic Integrity and remain on file for the duration of enrollment.

2.5 Class Attendance
Attendance policies are set by individual instructors and outlined in each course syllabus. University-wide guidelines include:
- Students must attend at least 80% of scheduled class sessions
- Absences exceeding 20% may result in administrative withdrawal from the course
- Excused absences include documented illness, family emergency, university-sponsored events, and religious observances
- Students must notify instructors within 48 hours of any absence
- Make-up work for excused absences must be completed within 7 calendar days

3. TUITION AND FINANCIAL AID

3.1 Tuition and Fees (Per Semester, 2025–2026)
In-State Students:
- Undergraduate: $5,850 per semester (12–18 credit hours)
- Graduate: $7,200 per semester (9–12 credit hours)
- Per-credit surcharge above maximum: $425/credit hour

Out-of-State Students:
- Undergraduate: $14,200 per semester (12–18 credit hours)
- Graduate: $16,800 per semester (9–12 credit hours)
- Per-credit surcharge above maximum: $975/credit hour

Mandatory Fees (included in tuition):
- Student Activity Fee: $350
- Technology Fee: $275
- Health Center Fee: $200
- Recreation Center Fee: $175
- Library Fee: $100

3.2 Payment Plans
PSU offers the following payment options:
- Full Payment: Due by the first day of classes each semester
- Monthly Installment Plan: 4 equal payments spread across the semester ($50 enrollment fee)
- Employer Reimbursement Deferment: Available for students with verified employer tuition programs
- Financial Aid Deferment: Automatic for students with pending financial aid awards

Late payment fees: $75 for the first late payment, $150 for subsequent late payments per semester.

3.3 Scholarships and Grants
Merit-Based Scholarships:
- Presidential Scholarship: Full tuition (GPA 3.9+, SAT 1400+/ACT 32+)
- Dean's Scholarship: $8,000/year (GPA 3.7+, SAT 1300+/ACT 29+)
- Pinnacle Achievement Award: $5,000/year (GPA 3.5+)
- Departmental Scholarships: Vary by college ($1,000–$4,000/year)

Need-Based Aid:
- Federal Pell Grant: Up to $7,395/year (based on FAFSA EFC)
- PSU Need-Based Grant: Up to $6,000/year
- Federal Work-Study: $3,000–$5,000/year (campus employment)

Scholarship Renewal Requirements:
- Maintain minimum GPA specified in the scholarship agreement (typically 3.0–3.5)
- Enroll full-time (12+ credit hours per semester)
- Submit scholarship renewal application by March 1 each year
- Complete 30 credit hours per academic year

3.4 Refund Policy
- Before classes begin: 100% refund
- Week 1–2: 80% refund
- Week 3–4: 60% refund
- Week 5–6: 40% refund
- After Week 6: No refund

4. STUDENT SERVICES

4.1 Academic Advising
Every student is assigned an academic advisor within their declared major. Advisors help with:
- Course selection and degree planning
- Major/minor declarations and changes
- Graduate school and career preparation
- Academic difficulty intervention

Advising appointments can be scheduled through MyPSU or by contacting your department's advising office. Walk-in hours are available Monday–Thursday, 10:00 AM – 3:00 PM.

4.2 Counseling and Psychological Services (CAPS)
CAPS provides free, confidential mental health services to all enrolled students:
- Individual counseling (up to 12 sessions per academic year)
- Group therapy sessions (anxiety, stress management, grief support)
- Crisis intervention (24/7 hotline: 1-800-555-CAPS)
- Psychiatric services (medication management with referral)
- Workshops: Stress management, mindfulness, time management

Location: Student Wellness Center, Room 210
Hours: Monday–Friday, 8:00 AM – 6:00 PM
After-hours crisis line: 1-800-555-CAPS

4.3 Student Health Center
The Student Health Center provides primary care services:
- General medical visits (no appointment needed for urgent care)
- Immunization verification and administration
- Women's health services
- Allergy clinic (Monday and Wednesday afternoons)
- Travel health consultations
- Lab services (blood work, strep tests, flu tests)

All enrolled students are covered by the Student Health Fee. Prescriptions and specialist referrals may incur additional costs. Students may opt into the PSU Student Health Insurance Plan ($1,850/semester) or provide proof of comparable coverage.

Location: Health Sciences Building, 1st Floor
Hours: Monday–Friday, 8:00 AM – 5:00 PM; Saturday, 9:00 AM – 1:00 PM

4.4 Disability Resource Center (DRC)
The DRC provides accommodations and support for students with documented disabilities:
- Extended testing time (1.5x or 2x standard time)
- Note-taking assistance
- Alternative format textbooks (audio, Braille, large print)
- Priority registration
- Accessible housing arrangements
- Assistive technology training

Students must submit documentation from a qualified professional and complete an intake meeting with a DRC coordinator. Accommodations are not retroactive — register as early as possible.

Location: Student Services Building, Room 150
Contact: drc@pinnaclestate.edu | Extension 3500

4.5 Career Development Center
Services available to current students and alumni (up to 2 years post-graduation):
- Resume and cover letter reviews
- Mock interviews and interview coaching
- Career fairs (Fall: September 25; Spring: February 18)
- Internship and job postings through Handshake platform
- Employer networking events
- Graduate school application workshops

Location: University Center, 3rd Floor
Hours: Monday–Friday, 9:00 AM – 5:00 PM

5. CAMPUS LIFE

5.1 Housing and Residence Life
On-Campus Housing Options:
- Traditional Residence Halls: Shared double rooms ($4,200/semester)
- Suite-Style Housing: 4-person suites with shared bathroom ($4,800/semester)
- Apartment-Style Housing: 2-bedroom apartments for upperclassmen ($5,400/semester)
- Graduate Housing: Studio and 1-bedroom apartments ($5,800/semester)

All first-year students are required to live on campus unless they reside with a parent/guardian within 30 miles of campus. Housing applications open February 1 for the following academic year.

Each residence hall has a Resident Advisor (RA) on every floor and a Hall Director on staff. Quiet hours are 10:00 PM – 8:00 AM Sunday–Thursday and 12:00 AM – 10:00 AM Friday–Saturday.

5.2 Dining Services
Meal Plan Options (per semester):
- Unlimited Plan: Unlimited dining hall access + $200 Flex Dollars ($3,100)
- 14-Meal Plan: 14 meals/week + $350 Flex Dollars ($2,800)
- 10-Meal Plan: 10 meals/week + $500 Flex Dollars ($2,400)
- Commuter Plan: 50 meals/semester + $300 Flex Dollars ($1,200)

Dining Locations:
- Summit Dining Hall (main): Monday–Friday 7:00 AM – 9:00 PM; Weekends 9:00 AM – 8:00 PM
- Crest Café (food court): Monday–Friday 10:00 AM – 10:00 PM
- The Grind (coffee shop): Daily 6:30 AM – 11:00 PM
- Mountain Market (convenience store): Daily 8:00 AM – 12:00 AM

All dining locations accommodate dietary needs including vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free, and halal options. Allergen information is posted at every station.

5.3 Student Organizations and Clubs
PSU has over 200 registered student organizations across these categories:
- Academic and Professional (35+ organizations)
- Cultural and Identity (25+ organizations)
- Greek Life (12 fraternities, 10 sororities)
- Recreation and Sports Clubs (30+ clubs)
- Service and Volunteering (20+ organizations)
- Arts and Performance (15+ groups)
- Media and Publications (8 organizations)
- Religious and Spiritual (12+ organizations)

To start a new organization, submit a petition with at least 10 student signatures, a faculty advisor, and a constitution to the Office of Student Engagement.

5.4 Athletics and Recreation
PSU competes in NCAA Division II as the Pinnacle State Mountaineers. Varsity sports include:
- Fall: Football, Soccer (M/W), Volleyball (W), Cross Country (M/W)
- Winter: Basketball (M/W), Swimming (M/W), Indoor Track (M/W)
- Spring: Baseball (M), Softball (W), Tennis (M/W), Outdoor Track (M/W)

The Recreation Center is free for all enrolled students and includes:
- Olympic-size swimming pool
- Climbing wall
- Group fitness classes (yoga, cycling, HIIT, Zumba — 40+ classes/week)
- Indoor basketball and volleyball courts
- Weight room and cardio floor

Recreation Center Hours: Monday–Friday 6:00 AM – 11:00 PM; Weekends 8:00 AM – 9:00 PM

6. CODE OF CONDUCT

6.1 Behavioral Expectations
All students are expected to:
- Treat others with dignity and respect
- Comply with all university policies, local, state, and federal laws
- Maintain honesty and integrity in all interactions
- Respect university property and the property of others
- Contribute to a safe and inclusive campus community

6.2 Prohibited Conduct
The following behaviors are subject to disciplinary action:
- Harassment, intimidation, or bullying (including cyberbullying)
- Physical violence or threats of violence
- Possession, use, or distribution of illegal substances on campus
- Underage alcohol consumption or providing alcohol to minors
- Theft or vandalism of university or personal property
- Unauthorized access to university facilities or systems
- Disruptive behavior in academic settings
- Hazing in any form (including by student organizations)
- Violation of the university's non-discrimination policy
- Possession of weapons on campus (including firearms, even with a permit)

6.3 Disciplinary Process
1. Incident Report: Filed by campus security, faculty, staff, or students
2. Investigation: Conducted by the Office of Student Conduct within 10 business days
3. Hearing: Student appears before the Student Conduct Board with the right to an advisor
4. Decision: Rendered within 5 business days of the hearing
5. Appeal: Students may appeal within 10 business days to the Dean of Students

Possible Sanctions:
- Written Warning
- Educational Sanctions (community service, reflection essays, workshops)
- Probation (specified period with behavioral conditions)
- Suspension (removal from university for 1–4 semesters)
- Expulsion (permanent removal from the university)

7. GRADUATION REQUIREMENTS

7.1 Undergraduate Degree Requirements
To earn a bachelor's degree from PSU, students must:
- Complete a minimum of 120 credit hours (some programs require more)
- Achieve a cumulative GPA of 2.0 or above
- Complete all General Education requirements (42 credit hours):
  - English Composition (6 credits)
  - Mathematics (3–6 credits depending on major)
  - Natural Sciences with lab (8 credits)
  - Social Sciences (6 credits)
  - Humanities and Fine Arts (6 credits)
  - Diversity and Global Perspectives (3 credits)
  - First-Year Seminar (3 credits)
  - Upper-Division Writing Intensive (3 credits)
- Complete all major-specific requirements
- Complete at least 30 of the final 40 credit hours at PSU (residency requirement)
- Satisfy all financial obligations to the university
- Apply for graduation by the posted deadline (October 1 for December, February 1 for May, June 1 for August)

7.2 Graduate Degree Requirements
Requirements vary by program but generally include:
- Master's Programs: 30–60 credit hours, cumulative GPA of 3.0+, thesis or comprehensive exam
- Doctoral Programs: 60–90 credit hours beyond the bachelor's, qualifying exams, dissertation defense
- All graduate students must complete their degree within 7 years of initial enrollment

7.3 Commencement
Commencement ceremonies are held three times per year:
- December Commencement: Second Saturday of December
- May Commencement: Third Saturday of May
- August Commencement: First Saturday of August

Students must apply for graduation and clear all financial holds to participate in commencement. Regalia (cap, gown, and tassel) must be ordered through the University Bookstore by the posted deadline.
